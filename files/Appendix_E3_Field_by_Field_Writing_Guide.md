# What to write in Appendix E3: Anecdotal Evidence Appraisal Tool

*Johns Hopkins Evidence-Based Practice for Nurses and Healthcare Professionals: Model & Guidelines*, fifth edition (2025)

This guide follows the **five-step Appendix E3 process** in the earlier [E1, E2, and E3 appraisal guide](https://chatgpt.com/library?file_id=libfile_a40f4fbc80408191937a85bf3b8c8b98&account_id=272b87f4-0126-403f-8878-95893d50a1d3). The steps are a practical workflow, not numbered steps in the textbook. Page numbers below are **printed textbook pages**. Replace bracketed examples with details verified in the source. Complete the first-page data collection row **after** the quality assessment.

| Step | Appendix E3 field or record | What to write | Where to read |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Confirm the route** | Record the source type in appraisal notes; use E3 only for an anecdotal source. | Identify the source by author, date, and title, then explain briefly why it lacks a **formal study design** or a sufficiently systematic pre-appraised evidence process. E3 covers expert opinions, position statements, book chapters, clinical case reports, informal programmatic experiences, and reviews without a systematic approach. A detailed **case report** is not automatically a formal **case study research** design, and a review should be classified by its actual search and appraisal methods, not by its title alone. If the report describes a formal single study or a systematic review/guideline, reroute it using Appendix D. Example: “`[citation]` describes one patient's course without a formal study design → E3 case report.” E3 sources offer **limited support for decision-making** if adequate to include. | **Chapter 6, pp. 87–89; Chapter 8, pp. 121, 129; Appendix D, pp. 226–227; Appendix E3, p. 244.** |
| **2. Choose the source-specific subsection** | Select one **Section I: Quality Appraisal** checklist. | Match the source to **Expert Opinion, Position Statements, and Book Chapters** (pp. 244–245); **Case Report** (p. 245); **Programmatic Experiences** (p. 246); or **Reviews with an Unsystematic Approach** (pp. 246–247). Write the chosen subtype in your appraisal record and complete its corresponding checklist. For a program report, establish whether the authors simply describe an implementation experience or actually report a formal study; for a review, inspect whether selection and appraisal of evidence were systematic. When methods establish another evidence type, return to Appendix D rather than forcing the source into E3. | **Chapter 6, pp. 87–89; Appendix E3, pp. 244–247.** |
| **3. Appraise the source** | In the selected Section I checklist, mark **Yes / No / Unclear / N/A** for the applicable questions; document important rationales in appraisal notes. | For **expert opinion, position statements, and book chapters**, assess the author group's expertise, stated purpose, engagement with relevant literature, logic of conclusions, clarity of recommendations, and funding/conflicts (pp. 244–245). For a **case report**, assess why the case matters, patient history and clinical details, diagnostic or treatment information, consent, whether the interpretation and lessons follow from the reported case, connections to relevant literature, readability, and funding/conflicts (p. 245). For a **programmatic experience**, assess purpose, setting and participants, what the team did, any planning framework, reported impact, lessons and improvement opportunities, whether its implementation and measurement can be followed, and funding/conflicts (p. 246). For an **unsystematic review**, assess rationale and question, any declared model, what authors intended to describe, how articles were selected, clarity of findings, whether discussion and conclusions follow, and funding/conflicts (pp. 246–247). Mark **Unclear** for information the source does not provide, and **N/A** only where a prompt genuinely does not apply. In notes, identify substantial gaps, their effect on trustworthiness, and the source page or section supporting your response. | **Chapter 8, pp. 122, 129; Appendix E3, pp. 244–247; Appendix F, pp. 250–263.** |
| **4. Decide adequacy** | Record an **adequate / inadequate** quality decision and reason in the appraisal or project log. | Consider the checklist answers together, especially whether the source describes its context clearly enough and whether its conclusions are supported by the material presented. After independent review and team discussion, write a short decision such as “**Adequate** for `[limited contextual insight]` because `[reported strengths]`; limitation: `[important gap]`” or “**Inadequate** because `[critical missing or inconsistent information]`.” The E3 checklist does **not** contain a separate final adequacy checkbox; record this decision in your notes or evidence log. Do not determine adequacy by a fixed number of Yes answers or use older A/B/C quality grades. Adequacy permits inclusion but **does not change the source's limited support level**. | **Chapter 8, pp. 120–123, 129; Appendix E3, pp. 244–247.** |
| **5. Record the outcome** | E3 **first-page data collection table** for retained sources; exclusion log for rejected sources. | If quality is **adequate**, complete the table from the source: **Article Number** (matching the project log); **Author, date, title**; **Type of evidence** (the actual E3 subtype); **Population, size, and setting** (or “not reported” where relevant); **Intervention** (or “not applicable”); **Findings that help answer the EBP question** (attribute reported observations or opinions to their authors without presenting them as proven effects); **Measures used** (if any); and **Limitations** (for example, single-case scope, missing measurements, or author selection of literature). The last column already specifies **Limited** support. If quality is **inadequate**, record the source and exclusion reason in the project log and omit its summary row. Keep this extraction faithful to the source; synthesis and recommendations happen later. | **Appendix E3, pp. 244–247; Chapter 9, pp. 134–135.** |

## Writing check

Verify that Appendix D routes the source to E3; only the matching subtype checklist was used; **No**, **Unclear**, and **N/A** responses that affect the decision are explained; and the adequate/inadequate judgment is documented separately from the preprinted **Limited** support level. Enter the first-page summary only for an included source. Its findings should be described at the strength the source actually supports, without inferring causality from anecdotal information.
