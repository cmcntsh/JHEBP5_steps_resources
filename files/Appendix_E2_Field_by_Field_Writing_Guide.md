# What to write in Appendix E2: Single Study Evidence Appraisal Tool

*Johns Hopkins Evidence-Based Practice for Nurses and Healthcare Professionals: Model & Guidelines*, fifth edition (2025)

This guide follows the **five-step Appendix E2 process** in the earlier [E1, E2, and E3 appraisal guide](https://chatgpt.com/library?file_id=libfile_a40f4fbc80408191937a85bf3b8c8b98&account_id=272b87f4-0126-403f-8878-95893d50a1d3). The steps are a practical workflow, not numbered steps in the textbook. Page numbers below are **printed textbook pages**. Replace bracketed examples with facts verified in the study. Complete the first-page data collection row **after** the quality assessment.

| Step | Appendix E2 field or record | What to write | Where to read |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Confirm a formal single-study design** | **Section I** decision tree: single study and formal study design; source-identification notes. | Identify the article by author, year, and title in the project record. Check whether it reports **one original study** using a defined design, with stated methods for gathering and analyzing data. Note the design the authors actually used and the features that support your classification; do not rely on a label in the title alone. If it is a review, guideline, case report, informal program description, or another source that does not meet both decision-tree conditions, return to **Appendix D** to select the appropriate tool. Example note: “`[citation]` reports one prospective cohort study with stated recruitment, measures, and analysis; proceed with E2.” | **Chapter 6, pp. 85–87; Appendix D, p. 227; Chapter 8, pp. 126–129; Appendix E2, p. 236.** |
| **2. Determine design-based support** | Complete **Section I: Level of Support for Practice Change** and record the resulting support level with a rationale. | Follow the actual decision tree: **Did the study team implement an intervention?** If **No**, a formal observational study routes to **moderate** support. If **Yes**, ask whether there was an **explicitly stated and analyzed control group**. An intervention with such a control group routes to **strong** support, whether or not allocation was randomized; an intervention without it routes to **moderate** support. Write the observed design and your reason in appraisal notes, for example: “Pre/post intervention; no distinct analyzed control group → moderate.” A comparison of naturally occurring groups by itself does not establish that investigators implemented a controlled intervention. The design gives the *potential* support level; adequate quality must still be established. | **Chapter 8, pp. 120–121, 126–129; Appendix D, p. 227; Appendix E2, p. 236.** |
| **3. Select the quality section** | Choose **Section IIA (Qualitative)**, **IIB (Quantitative)**, or **IIC (Mixed Methods)**. | Record which approach the study **actually uses to collect and analyze its data**. Select **IIA** for a qualitative inquiry (such as interviews analyzed for themes), **IIB** for quantitative measurement and statistical analysis, or **IIC** when both qualitative and quantitative methods are genuinely used and integrated. Do not infer “mixed methods” merely because a questionnaire contains a comment box or an article mentions both numbers and quotations. Record the selected section in appraisal notes, then complete **only that checklist**. | **Chapter 6, pp. 85–87; Appendix E2, pp. 237–242.** |
| **4. Work through the applicable questions** | The chosen Section II checklist: **Yes / No / Unclear / N/A** for each applicable item; notes on consequential concerns. | Mark each item from the reported methods and findings, checking supplements if needed. For **IIA, qualitative** (pp. 237–238), assess the aim and rationale, setting, sampling and eligibility, sample adequacy or saturation, reflexivity, data gathering and processing, analysis, credibility of themes and supporting observations or quotations, ethics, limitations, and consistency. For **IIB, quantitative** (pp. 238–240), assess design, setting, sampling and eligibility, sample-size justification, specified measures and their validity or reliability, analysis, intervention description, randomization and group treatment if applicable, participant characteristics, attrition and response rates if applicable, baseline balance, results and statistics, ethics, and limitations. For **IIC, mixed methods** (pp. 240–242), assess each component's sampling, sample adequacy, collection, processing, analysis and findings **and whether the qualitative and quantitative strands are actually integrated**. For all sections, check whether conclusions match results and whether funding or conflicts are addressed. Use **Unclear** when a needed fact is not reported and **N/A** only when the item genuinely does not apply; briefly explain significant No, Unclear, or N/A judgments in appraisal notes with article page references. Do not convert a missing report into a Yes. | **Chapter 8, pp. 122, 126–129; Appendix E2, qualitative pp. 237–238, quantitative pp. 238–240, mixed methods pp. 240–242; Appendix F, pp. 250–263.** |
| **5. Decide adequacy, then collect data** | Final quality decision in appraisal notes or project log; E2 **first-page data collection table**. | After independent review and team discussion, record **adequate** or **inadequate** quality and a brief explanation of the decisive strengths and weaknesses. The form's checklist has no separate final letter-grade field: do **not** assign an old A/B/C grade or decide adequacy by counting Yes answers. If inadequate, note the exclusion and do not populate an inclusion summary row. If adequate, fill the first-page row **from the article**: **Article Number** (consistent with the project log); **Author, date, title**; **Type of evidence** (specific design); **Population, size, and setting**; **Intervention** (or “not applicable” for a noninterventional study); **Findings that help answer the EBP question** (direction and actual result, without implying causation beyond the design); **Measures used** (instruments, indicators, or outcome definitions); **Limitations** (especially those affecting interpretation or applicability); and **Level of support for decision-making** from Step 2. Keep the design-based support level distinct from this quality decision. | **Chapter 8, pp. 120–123, 126–129; Appendix E2, pp. 236–242; Chapter 9, pp. 134–135.** |

## Writing check

Verify that the source is a formal single study; the Section I route reflects whether investigators **introduced an intervention and analyzed a stated control group**; and the chosen Section II checklist matches the study's methods. Explain consequential uncertainties, record a reasoned adequate/inadequate quality decision, and populate the first-page row only for retained evidence. The findings and limitations should match the article, and the support level should follow the design rather than the apparent size or favorability of its effect.
