# What to write in Appendix G2: Individual Evidence Summary Tool

*Johns Hopkins Evidence-Based Practice for Nurses and Healthcare Professionals: Model & Guidelines*, fifth edition (2025)

This guide follows the **seven-step Appendix G2 process** in the earlier [G1 and G2 completion guide](https://chatgpt.com/library?file_id=libfile_36846b3a2d7081919edeeed2bb3c13b9&account_id=272b87f4-0126-403f-8878-95893d50a1d3). The steps are a practical workflow, not numbered steps in the textbook. Page numbers below are **printed textbook pages**. Replace bracketed examples with information verified in the source. G2 is the individual-source table for an **exhaustive evidence search**. Record what each included source reports; compare sources and develop the team's recommendations in the subsequent synthesis step.

| Step | Appendix G2 field or record | What to write | Where to read |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Confirm the G2 pathway** | Preparation for G2; keep the **EBP question, Appendix C search and screening record**, and the included-source list beside the table. | Verify that Appendix C documents an **exhaustive search** and screening against the current EBP question and inclusion/exclusion criteria. Assemble the citations that passed screening, including any relevant pre-appraised sources that do not fully answer the question, formal single studies, and anecdotal sources. Note in the project record the search date, eligibility decision, and source identifier for each item. G2 has no separate field for a search narrative. If the team followed the best-evidence and targeted-update route using suitable pre-appraised evidence, consult G1 instead. | **Chapter 7, pp. 98–113; Chapter 9, pp. 134–135; Appendix B, pp. 212–215; Appendix C, pp. 218–223; Appendix G2, p. 272.** |
| **2. Finish appraisals and reconcile inclusion** | Before entering a G2 row, retain the applicable **Appendix E1, E2, or E3 appraisal** and reviewers' decision record. | For every screened source, use Appendix D to choose its appraisal tool; record its **relevance**, **adequate or inadequate quality decision**, and the reason for that judgment. For pre-appraised evidence, document how much of the EBP question it actually addresses; for formal studies, apply E2; for anecdotal evidence, apply E3. Independent reviewers should settle any disagreement about including a source and about what it reports. Enter **only relevant, adequate-quality sources** in G2. Keep excluded citations and reasons in a separate log so screening and appraisal exclusions remain traceable. | **Chapter 8, pp. 119–129; Chapter 9, p. 135; Appendix D, pp. 226–227; Appendices E1–E3, pp. 230–247.** |
| **3. Record identification and design** | G2: **Article number; Reviewer names; Author, date, and title; Type of evidence**. | Assign a **unique article number** used in all project records. Name the reviewers who actually read the source. Enter the **first author's surname**, publication date, and full identifying title (or the producing organization where that is the appropriate citation). Describe the evidence type accurately: for example, “systematic review,” “qualitative descriptive study,” “nonrandomized controlled trial,” “case report,” or “programmatic experience,” depending on the source. The p. 274 word bank can help with review type, methodology, design/approach, and timing; choose **only applicable descriptors**, and do not infer a formal study design from a title alone. Example: “`[article #] | [reviewers] | [first author, year, title] | prospective observational cohort`.” | **Chapter 6, pp. 81–89; Appendix F, pp. 250–263; Appendix G2, pp. 272–274.** |
| **4. Record population and intervention** | G2: **Population, size, and setting; Intervention**. | State **who or what was studied**, the actual sample size or number of observations, and where the work occurred; add relevant characteristics or location details that affect how well it answers the EBP question. Describe any **intervention, exposure, comparator, or program activity as reported**, including a distinguishing element such as frequency or delivery if important. For an observational study, descriptive report, or anecdotal source that implemented no intervention, say **“No investigator-implemented intervention; focus: `[topic/exposure]`”** instead of inventing treatment. Avoid simply repeating the team's question; give the source's actual population and approach. | **Appendix B, pp. 212–215; Chapter 9, pp. 134–135; Appendix G2, pp. 272–273.** |
| **5. Extract question-relevant findings and measures** | G2: **Findings that help answer the EBP question; Measures used**. | In **Findings**, summarize the source's specific results, recommendations, or reported observations that address the question. Include direction, comparison, outcome and useful numerical results when the source reports them; mention null or conflicting findings where relevant. Attribute an opinion or case observation as such, and avoid presenting an association as a proven intervention effect. In **Measures used**, name the instruments, outcome definitions, data sources, or qualitative methods the authors used to assess the result; **do not repeat the result in this column**. Example: “Findings: `[reported difference or theme and context]`; Measures: `[named scale, audit indicator, interview method, or data source]`.” If a source has no formal measures, write **“No formal measure reported”** rather than supplying one. | **Chapter 9, pp. 134–135; Appendix G2, pp. 272–273.** |
| **6. Document limitations and support** | G2: **Limitations; Level of support for decision-making; Notes to the team**. | In **Limitations**, combine relevant author-reported caveats with material appraisal concerns, such as selection bias, confounding, missing outcomes, attrition, incomplete methods, or limited transferability. Explain the likely effect on interpreting this particular finding; note that a design-inherent limitation need not by itself make the source inadequate. In **Level of support**, record the category justified by Appendix D and the appraisal: an adequately appraised pre-appraised source that answers only part of the question may offer **strong** support; formal single studies offer **strong** or **moderate** support according to design; and adequate anecdotal evidence remains **limited**. An adequate-quality decision does **not** by itself raise the support level. If fully suitable pre-appraised evidence provides **independent** support, check whether the G1 best-evidence pathway applies instead. In **Notes to the team**, add issues helpful during synthesis that do not fit elsewhere, such as overlapping data, a subgroup or setting mismatch, or a contradiction needing review. Example: “Potential confounding by `[factor]`; findings apply only to `[subgroup]`; compare with article `[number]`.” | **Chapter 8, pp. 120–129; Appendix D, pp. 226–227; Appendices E1–E3, pp. 230–247; Appendix G2, pp. 272–273.** |
| **7. Check the table and move to synthesis** | Final G2 row audit; prepare **Appendix H, Sections I and II**. | Compare every row with its full source and appraisal: ensure all included sources have one complete row, numbers and reviewer names match project records, findings address the current EBP question, **measures are distinct from results**, limitations are accurate, and support levels are justified. Resolve disagreement before finalizing. Reconcile G2 inclusions with the Appendix C screening count **and any later quality-based exclusions**, keeping the reasons for changes. Then group rows by support level and compare findings across sources for **Appendix H Section I**; use the resulting synthesis to write question-answering recommendations and their certainty **in Section II**. Do not substitute a row's individual finding for the team's cross-source synthesis. | **Chapter 9, pp. 134–135, 137–145; Appendix G2, pp. 272–273; Appendix H, pp. 278–282.** |

## Writing check

Confirm that the documented search was exhaustive and every G2 source was eligible and adequately appraised. Each row identifies its reviewer(s), describes the **actual** source design and sample, extracts only question-relevant findings, distinguishes measures from findings, reports meaningful limitations, and assigns support for decision-making separately from quality adequacy. Keep excluded-source decisions in the project log; use the retained rows for Appendix H synthesis and recommendations.
