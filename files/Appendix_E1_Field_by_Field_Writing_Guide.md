# What to write in Appendix E1: Pre-Appraised Evidence Appraisal Tool

*Johns Hopkins Evidence-Based Practice for Nurses and Healthcare Professionals: Model & Guidelines*, fifth edition (2025)

This guide follows the **five-step Appendix E1 process** in the earlier [E1, E2, and E3 appraisal guide](https://chatgpt.com/library?file_id=libfile_a40f4fbc80408191937a85bf3b8c8b98&account_id=272b87f4-0126-403f-8878-95893d50a1d3). The steps are a practical workflow, not numbered steps in the textbook. Page numbers are **printed textbook pages**. Replace bracketed examples with details verified in the source being appraised. The first-page collection table is completed **after** the applicable appraisal.

| Step | Appendix E1 field or record | What to write | Where to read |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Confirm the route** | Select the applicable type of pre-appraised evidence and its **Section II** checklist; identify the source in working notes. | Determine whether the source is an **evidence summary**, **clinical practice guideline (CPG)**, or **literature review with a systematic approach (LRSA)**. Check its *methods*, not just its title: a review needs a systematic approach, and a guideline should be based on a rigorous evidence process. Note its complete citation, the chosen subtype, and why it belongs on E1 rather than E2 or E3. For example: “`[author/organization, year]`: CPG; documented evidence search and recommendations.” Select only the corresponding quality checklist. Leave the first-page collection row until Step 5. | **Chapter 6, pp. 81–85; Chapter 8, pp. 122–123; Appendix D, p. 226; Appendix E1, pp. 230–232.** |
| **2. Establish the search pathway** | **Section I: Suitability** instructions; record the route in project notes or the evidence log. | State whether the source is being considered as **potential independent support** in a best-evidence search or was retrieved in an **exhaustive search**. For potential independent support, complete **Section I and Section II**. For a source gathered through an exhaustive search, follow E1's instruction to **skip Section I and start at Section II**. E1 has no separate box labeled “search pathway,” so keep a note such as “`[source]` found in `[best-evidence/exhaustive]` search; Section I `[completed/skipped]`.” For an exhaustive route, confirm that eligibility screening was already performed. | **Chapter 7, pp. 109–113; Appendix C, pp. 218–223; Chapter 8, pp. 122–126; Appendix E1, p. 230.** |
| **3. Assess suitability when required** | Section I checks for **topic/intervention, population, setting, outcomes if applicable, age of references, and reasonable currency**; **Notes**. | Compare each element of the **current Appendix B EBP question** with the source. Mark **Yes, No, Unclear, or N/A** where the form provides these choices, and explain meaningful differences in **Notes**. For “How recent are the references?”, supply an actual date or date range from the underlying evidence and, when identifiable, the **date the source's literature search ended**; distinguish that date from the guideline or review's publication year. For “recent enough,” explain whether any change in practice or research makes the underlying evidence too old for this topic. If the question has no outcome to match, mark that item N/A and explain why. An independently actionable source must meet the applicable suitability criteria; if it covers only part of the question, document the gap and proceed with quality appraisal if it still informs the question. Example note: “`[source]` includes `[population]` but not `[setting]`; applicability to `[setting]` is uncertain.” | **Appendix B, pp. 212–215; Chapter 8, pp. 123–126; Appendix E1, p. 230.** |
| **4. Appraise quality** | **Section II: Quality Appraisal**—complete the one checklist matching the subtype. | Mark every applicable item **Yes, No, Unclear, or N/A** based on the actual report, its supplement, or the producer's published methods. For an **evidence summary** (p. 231), assess the organization's reputation; systematic selection and evaluation methods; transparent question and included evidence; the link from evidence to recommendations; stated confidence or certainty; peer review; and funding or conflicts. For a **CPG** (pp. 231–232), assess the review group's expertise, intended population, recommendation process, clarity and certainty of recommendations, external review, alignment of recommendation certainty with evidence, and conflicts. Inspect the underlying evidence-review methods as needed. For an **LRSA** (pp. 232–233), work through background/question, reproducible search and selection, independent checking, study-level appraisal and bias, synthesis methods, flow and study details, results, interpretation, consistency, limitations, and funding/conflicts. Write a short explanation for consequential **No**, **Unclear**, and **N/A** responses in the available notes/working appraisal record, with a source page or methods link. A missing detail remains **Unclear** unless checked in a legitimate supplement or organizational methods page. | **Chapter 8, pp. 120–123, 125–126; Appendix E1, pp. 231–233; Appendix F, pp. 250–263.** |
| **5. Make and record the decision** | Appraisal notes or evidence log for the quality decision; **first-page data collection table**; later evidence summary. | After reviewers discuss the checklist, record **adequate** or **inadequate** quality with a concise reason, including any important limitations. E1 provides checklist responses but no separate final quality-grade box: retain the decision in appraisal notes or the evidence log. There is **no A/B/C grade or required count of Yes answers**. If inadequate, record the exclusion and stop. If adequate, complete the first-page row: **Article Number** matching the project log; **Author, date, title**; **Type of pre-appraised evidence**; **Topic or intervention**; **Population**; **Setting**; and the source's **recommendations that answer this EBP question**. Preserve the author's own wording or strength of recommendations accurately when summarizing; do not add findings not in the source. When Section I establishes full suitability and quality is adequate, document potential **independent support** and conduct the targeted update search. If adequate and relevant but only partly suitable, explain the gap: it may inform the project as **strong support** in G2 while the team continues the exhaustive route. | **Chapter 8, pp. 121–126; Appendix E1, pp. 230, 233; Chapter 9, pp. 134–135.** |

## Writing check

Confirm that the subtype and checklist match the source; Section I was completed **only on the independent-support route**; dates describe the age of the *underlying evidence*, not simply the source's publication date; and every consequential negative or unclear appraisal answer has a traceable rationale. The quality decision should agree with the checklist, and any first-page recommendations should relate directly to the current EBP question. Distinguish **suitability**, **quality adequacy**, and **level of support** in the project record.
