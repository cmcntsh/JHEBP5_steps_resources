# What to write in Appendix G1: Best-Evidence Summary Tool

*Johns Hopkins Evidence-Based Practice for Nurses and Healthcare Professionals: Model & Guidelines*, fifth edition (2025)

This guide follows the **six-step Appendix G1 process** in the earlier [G1 and G2 completion guide](https://chatgpt.com/library?file_id=libfile_36846b3a2d7081919edeeed2bb3c13b9&account_id=272b87f4-0126-403f-8878-95893d50a1d3). The steps are a practical workflow, not numbered steps in the textbook. Page numbers below are **printed textbook pages**. Replace bracketed examples with facts verified in the included sources. G1 summarizes evidence from a **best-evidence search** and, if needed, a **targeted update search**; its rows should extract findings from sources rather than contain the team's synthesis or recommendations.

| Step | Appendix G1 field or record | What to write | Where to read |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Confirm the G1 pathway** | Preparation for G1; check the search and appraisal records. | Locate the team's current EBP question, Appendix C search record, and completed E1 appraisals. Record in project notes which **pre-appraised source(s)** from the best-evidence search were judged suitable for the entire question and of adequate quality. Note each source's **underlying literature-search date** and whether a targeted search since that date yielded newer evidence. For example: “`[guideline/review]` covers `[population, setting, topic]`; E1 suitable and adequate; underlying search ended `[date]`; targeted update ran `[date]`.” G1 has no separate pathway narrative field, so keep this record with the project files. If the team instead conducted an exhaustive search, consult the G2 pathway. | **Chapter 7, pp. 95–98; Chapter 8, pp. 122–126; Chapter 9, pp. 134–135; Appendix C, pp. 218–223; Appendix E1, pp. 230–233.** |
| **2. Agree on included sources** | Final inclusion list and reviewers' working notes, before entering G1 rows. | Compare the EBP question and eligibility decisions with each candidate's completed appraisal. Write the **citation, reason for inclusion, suitability, adequate-quality judgment, and unresolved issue if any** in an evidence log. For an excluded item, retain its citation and reason outside G1; do not enter an inadequate-quality item in the summary. Ask independent reviewers to reconcile differences about which sources belong and what each source reports before the summary is finalized. A broad guideline may be included for its question-relevant recommendations even though the rest of its guidance addresses other populations or topics. | **Chapter 8, pp. 120–126; Chapter 9, p. 135; Appendix D, p. 226; Appendix E1, pp. 230–233.** |
| **3. Fill Section I: Pre-Appraised Evidence** | For **each included source**, complete **Article Number; Author (organization), date, title; Type of pre-appraised evidence; Topic or Intervention; Population; Setting; Recommendations that answer the EBP question**. | Give each resource a **unique article number** used consistently throughout the project. Identify its actual organization or authors, publication date, and title; classify it as a **clinical practice guideline, literature review with a systematic approach, or evidence summary**. State the topic or intervention *as covered in that source*, then its intended population and setting; record a wider scope faithfully rather than silently narrowing it to the team's setting. In the final column, paraphrase or accurately summarize **only the source's recommendations that directly answer the current EBP question**, indicating recommendation certainty or strength where the source gives it. Example: “`[Source]` recommends `[specific action]` for `[relevant population]` (`[reported recommendation strength]`).” Do not insert the team's own recommendation or reproduce an entire guideline. | **Appendix B, pp. 212–215; Chapter 9, pp. 134–135; Appendix G1, pp. 266, 268.** |
| **4. Decide whether Section II is needed** | G1 p. 267: **“Was there additional evidence identified in the targeted search?”** | Select **No** when the targeted update identified **no additional eligible evidence for G1 Section II**, and follow the form's direction to **Section II of Appendix H**. Select **Yes** when it found additional relevant evidence that can be appraised and included, then complete G1 Section II for sources with **adequate quality and strong or moderate support for decision-making**. Note in the separate search or exclusion log any hits that were irrelevant, inadequate, or only limited support, so the checkbox and the absence of rows are understandable. A targeted search itself does not turn its findings into a new exhaustive-search summary. | **Chapter 7, p. 98; Chapter 9, p. 135; Appendix G1, p. 267; Appendix H, pp. 278–282.** |
| **5. Fill Section II: Reports of Single Studies From the Targeted Evidence Search** | For **each included targeted-search study**, complete **Article number; Reviewer names; Author, date, and title; Type of evidence; Population, size, and setting; Intervention; Findings that help answer the EBP question; Measures used; Limitations; Moderate or strong support for decision-making**. | Assign a unique article number and name the reviewers who read the report; cite the **first author, date, and title**. Describe the study design precisely, using the p. 270 word bank only where its descriptors apply. Enter the actual population, **sample size**, and setting; describe the intervention or comparison *as reported*, or identify the study focus if there was no implemented intervention. In **Findings**, summarize the question-relevant results in your own words with their direction and relevant numbers where available. In **Measures**, list the instruments, data sources, or indicators used to obtain those results—**not the results themselves**. In **Limitations**, record relevant author-reported concerns plus material flaws identified during appraisal, including implications for applicability. Enter **Strong** only when E2's design criteria support it; otherwise enter **Moderate** for an eligible formal study, and retain only adequately appraised sources. Example: “Findings: `[group]` had `[reported outcome/result]`; Measures: `[named scale or clinical indicator]`.” | **Chapter 8, pp. 126–129; Chapter 9, p. 135; Appendix E2, pp. 236–242; Appendix F, pp. 250–263; Appendix G1, pp. 267, 269–270.** |
| **6. Review the completed table** | Final check of both G1 sections and handoff to **Appendix H, Section II**. | Compare every G1 cell with the source and completed appraisal: confirm the citation and article number match project records; recommendations and findings address the **same current EBP question**; sample and setting are accurate; **Measures** names how outcomes were assessed while **Findings** states the outcomes; and limitations and support level agree with appraisal. Have reviewers resolve ambiguous entries before treating G1 as complete. If a particular intervention detail matters to implementation (for example, frequency or delivery), add a clearly labeled column or team note and extract that detail from the source. Then carry the supported recommendations and any additional findings forward for the **Appendix H, Section II** best-evidence recommendations step; do not write cross-source synthesis into a G1 source row. | **Chapter 9, pp. 134–136; Appendix G1, pp. 268–269; Appendix H, pp. 278–282.** |

## Writing check

G1 Section I contains **suitable, adequately appraised pre-appraised evidence**. If Section II is used, its rows are **adequately appraised targeted-search studies** providing strong or moderate support; limited-support and inadequate-quality sources do not go in that table. Every row is a faithful, question-relevant extraction, and the team's final best-evidence recommendations are developed in Appendix H rather than attributed to the original sources.
