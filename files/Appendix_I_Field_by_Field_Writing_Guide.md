# What to write in Appendix I: Translation Tool

*Johns Hopkins Evidence-Based Practice for Nurses and Healthcare Professionals: Model & Guidelines*, fifth edition (2025)

This guide follows the **eight-step Appendix I process** in the earlier [Appendix I completion guide](https://chatgpt.com/library?file_id=libfile_33ca2fb42e1c81919d5c4707b8260748&account_id=272b87f4-0126-403f-8878-95893d50a1d3). The steps are a practical workflow, not numbered steps in the textbook. Page numbers below are **printed textbook pages**. Replace bracketed examples with verified information about your organization. Appendix I presents **assessment prompts** for each recommendation and a **final space** for concise organization-specific recommendations; keep detailed assessment notes with the project record where the form has no dedicated answer field.

| Step | Appendix I field or record | What to write | Where to read |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Bring forward the recommendations** | Working assessment list; refer to **Appendix H** and the EBP question before completing I. | Copy or accurately identify **each distinct best-evidence recommendation** from Appendix H, together with its **own** high, reasonable, reasonable-to-low, or low certainty tier. Record the exact current EBP question and the local setting where each recommendation might apply. Number or label the items so the team can trace each I decision back to H. Consult G1 or G2 to clarify the population, intervention, and outcomes actually covered by the underlying evidence. Example working line: “H recommendation `[number/text]`; certainty `[tier]`; proposed setting `[unit/service]`; relevant population `[group]`.” Appendix I does not supply a separate source-import table, so keep this list alongside it. | **Chapter 9, pp. 139–142; Chapter 10, pp. 148–149; Appendix B, pp. 212–215; Appendix G1, pp. 266–270, or Appendix G2, pp. 272–274; Appendix H, pp. 279–282; Appendix I, p. 286.** |
| **2. Assess certainty** | Appendix I **Certainty** prompt; working note for each H recommendation. | For each item, record its **Appendix H certainty tier** and whether it is **high or reasonable**, the levels the tool identifies as supporting consideration of a practice change. For a **reasonable-to-low or low** tier, state that the evidence does not adequately support changing current practice and note the next option: wait for better evidence, consider research to fill a gap, or discontinue the proposed project. A high or reasonable tier moves the recommendation into local risk and context review; it is not by itself a claim that the intervention is safe or feasible in this organization. Example: “`[H item]`: reasonable certainty; assess local risks and readiness” or “`[H item]`: low certainty; no change proposed pending `[specific evidence need]`.” | **Chapter 9, pp. 139–142; Chapter 10, pp. 149–150; Appendix H, pp. 279–282; Appendix I, pp. 286–287.** |
| **3. Assess safety risk** | Appendix I **Risk** prompt; project risk-assessment notes. | Name the **specific potential harm or negative impact** on patients, staff, or the environment if this recommendation were introduced. Describe who could be affected, how likely or serious the concern appears from available local information, and **how the team could mitigate it**, without inventing a formal score. State if additional safety review is needed. Weigh the risk against the *recommendation's* certainty: a more hazardous change requires more compelling evidence; a small test may be considered where the evidence and risk profile support it. Example: “Possible risk: `[defined safety event]` for `[group]`; safeguard: `[protocol/training/monitoring]`; residual concern: `[issue]`.” | **Chapter 10, p. 150; Appendix I, pp. 286–287.** |
| **4. Assess fit** | Appendix I **Fit** prompt; notes on current practice and local alignment. | Describe the present workflow or policy and explain whether the proposed change aligns with the organization's and end users' **values, norms, goals, and skills**. Identify the actual points of agreement or friction: “`[practice]` fits `[existing workflow/goal]` but conflicts with `[specific process]`.” If an adaptation is needed, name it and check that it preserves the **essential evidence-supported intervention**, rather than changing it so much that the underlying recommendation no longer applies. Use G1/G2 when needed to check that the adapted population or intervention still matches what the evidence evaluated. | **Chapter 10, p. 151; Appendix I, pp. 286–287.** |
| **5. Assess feasibility** | Appendix I **Feasibility** prompts; notes on readiness, barriers, and resources. | State whether the proposed change is **doable here**, specifying the setting's readiness, who would perform it, required skills or training, staffing, equipment/materials, time, budget, and decision-maker support. Identify concrete barriers, who can address them, and whether they are realistic to overcome. Ask frontline staff and other affected groups when their input is needed to judge feasibility. Example: “Requires `[roles/training/materials]`; `[resource]` is available; `[barrier]` must be resolved by `[role]` before launch.” Distinguish an **unknown** resource or capacity from one confirmed to be available. | **Chapter 10, pp. 151–152; Appendix I, pp. 286–287.** |
| **6. Assess acceptability** | Appendix I **Acceptability** prompts; notes from impacted groups and leaders. | Identify **which patients, end users, staff, and leaders were consulted** and what each group considers acceptable, problematic, or conditional about the proposed practice. State whether leadership supports the change and regards it as reasonable, and whether it aligns with organizational priorities. Record material objections and the conditions needed for support; do not assume agreement because a group was not asked. Example: “`[group]` supports `[aspect]`; `[group]` raises `[concern]`; leadership support is `[confirmed/pending]` subject to `[condition]`.” Keep stakeholder feedback distinguishable from evidence-based certainty. | **Chapter 10, pp. 151–152; Appendix I, pp. 286–287.** |
| **7. Record the organization-specific recommendation** | Appendix I's final space: **concise organization-specific recommendations that address the EBP question**. | For **each H recommendation**, weigh **certainty, safety risk, fit, feasibility, and acceptability together**, then write a short, actionable decision for **this organization**. State the practice, target group and setting, and any essential condition or adaptation. Example: “For `[population]` in `[unit]`, adopt `[evidence-supported practice]` with `[essential safeguard]`, contingent on `[resource or approval]`.” If the evidence is inadequate or a risk or barrier prevents change, write a **specific defer/stop decision and why**, such as “Defer `[practice]` until `[evidence or readiness condition]` is met.” If the review shows the current practice lacks evidence or compelling evidence opposes it, a **de-implementation** recommendation may be appropriate. Keep this final field brief and preserve the detailed reasoning in the working assessment notes. | **Chapter 10, pp. 148–153; Appendix H, pp. 279–282; Appendix I, pp. 286–287.** |
| **8. Prepare the handoff** | Final review of Appendix I; transfer approved organization-specific recommendations into **Appendix J planning** when indicated. | Check that every organization-specific decision addresses the same EBP question, traces to an H recommendation, and is consistent with the recorded risk and local assessments. Review the wording and any **conditional prerequisites** with the team and relevant impacted groups. For a decision to proceed, carry the specific practice, population, setting, safeguards, resources, and unresolved conditions into implementation planning; **Appendix J** is where the team develops the framework, action plan, assignments, measures, and timeline. For a defer, research, or stop decision, retain the reason and what would trigger a reassessment. | **Chapter 10, p. 153; Chapter 11, pp. 157–159; Appendix I, pp. 286–287; Appendix J, pp. 290–294.** |

## Writing check

For each relevant Appendix H recommendation, confirm the team has considered **certainty, safety risk, fit, feasibility, and acceptability** before writing a local decision. Recommendations with **reasonable-to-low or low certainty do not provide adequate support for changing current practice**. Ensure the final Appendix I statements are specific to the setting and traceable to H, and that important constraints, reasons to defer, or grounds to discontinue a current practice are explicit. Detailed implementation tasks belong in the subsequent Appendix J plan.
