# What to write in Appendix H: Summary, Synthesis, & Best-Evidence Recommendations Tool

*Johns Hopkins Evidence-Based Practice for Nurses and Healthcare Professionals: Model & Guidelines*, fifth edition (2025)

This guide follows the **nine-step Appendix H process** in the earlier [Appendix H completion guide](https://chatgpt.com/library?file_id=libfile_ee05a73b978c81918ca447882052aacd&account_id=272b87f4-0126-403f-8878-95893d50a1d3). The steps are a practical workflow, not numbered steps in the textbook. Page numbers are **printed textbook pages**. Replace bracketed examples with statements supported by your own evidence. **G2 after an exhaustive search: complete Sections I and II. G1 after a best-evidence search, with or without a targeted update: skip Section I and complete Section II.**

| Step | Appendix H field or record | What to write | Where to read |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Confirm the question and search pathway** | Preparation for H; determine whether **Section I** applies. | Place the **current, exact EBP question** from Appendix B, the search pathway documented in Appendix C, and the completed **G1 or G2** table beside H. In project notes, record “`[EBP question]`; `[best-evidence/targeted or exhaustive]` search; `[G1 or G2]` summary.” If the team completed an **exhaustive search and G2**, plan to write synthesis in Section I and recommendations in Section II. If it used **G1**, leave H Section I unused and begin with Section II; the G1 table contains the pre-appraised recommendations and any targeted-search studies. H has no separate field for restating the question or pathway outside Section II's evidence-basis checkboxes. | **Chapter 7, pp. 93–98, 109–113; Chapter 9, pp. 134–135, 139–140; Appendix B, pp. 212–215; Appendix C, pp. 218–223; Appendix G1, pp. 266–270; Appendix G2, pp. 272–274; Appendix H, pp. 278–281.** |
| **2. Check the evidence entering H** | Review completed **G1/G2** and appraisals; document corrections in the evidence summary or project log. | Confirm that every source carried forward is relevant to the question and was judged **adequate quality**, with its **support for decision-making** classified consistently with Appendix D and the applicable E1, E2, or E3 appraisal. Check source numbers, populations, intervention details, findings, measures, and limitations. Ask reviewers to reconcile disagreements and correct the **source table** before using it in H. Maintain excluded or inadequate-quality sources and reasons in the separate project record; do not treat them as included support. This is a preparation step, not a request to copy each G1/G2 row into H. | **Chapter 8, pp. 120–129; Chapter 9, pp. 134–135, 139; Appendix D, pp. 226–227; Appendices E1–E3, pp. 230–247.** |
| **3. Organize the findings** | Working evidence matrix or notes before writing H. | For a **G2** project, group included sources by **strong, moderate, and limited** support for decision-making, then compare results within each group. Identify where sources agree, disagree, find no effect, differ by population or setting, or answer different aspects of the question. Preserve article numbers beside each observation. For a **G1** project, compare any targeted-search findings with the specific pre-appraised recommendations they might confirm, qualify, or challenge. Example working note: “`[article numbers]` address `[outcome]` in `[population]`; `[numbers]` report a different direction or no difference.” These sorting notes prepare the synthesis; they do not themselves constitute a recommendation. | **Chapter 9, pp. 137–139; Appendix G1, pp. 266–269; Appendix G2, pp. 272–273; Appendix H, pp. 278, 280.** |
| **4. Complete Section I if using G2** | **Section I: Strong, Moderate, and Limited** rows; **“Number of sources =”** and **“Synthesized Findings with Article Number(s).”** | Count **distinct included sources** in each support category and enter the number in that row. For each category, write short **cross-source statements** that answer an aspect of the EBP question: state the shared finding, meaningful differences or uncertainty, and a relevant population or outcome when needed. Attach the **article numbers actually supporting each statement**, for example: “Across `[population]`, `[pattern of outcome]` was reported under `[circumstances]` (`[article #s]`); `[contrary or null result]` occurred in `[subgroup]` (`[article #s]`).” Repeat for strong, moderate, and limited evidence, including negative or conflicting findings rather than selecting only favorable results. Do **not** paste the G2 rows or call a single article's finding a cross-source pattern. If a category has no included sources, enter **0** and leave its findings blank or state “No included sources.” This section is **not completed for G1**. | **Chapter 9, pp. 138–139; Appendix G2, pp. 272–273; Appendix H, pp. 278, 280.** |
| **5. Use the optional further-synthesis area when helpful** | **“Further Synthesis Based on Additional Organization and Analysis (OPTIONAL)”** in Section I. | Use this area **only for a G2 project when additional sorting yields a meaningful insight** beyond the three support rows. Write a concise comparison by, for example, population subgroup, setting, intervention component, delivery method, outcome, or contrasting findings. Give the supporting article numbers and state what the comparison adds: “`[intervention]` showed `[pattern]` among `[subgroup]` (`[numbers]`), whereas evidence in `[other subgroup]` was `[pattern/insufficient]` (`[numbers]`).” Leave the field blank when no additional analysis adds value. It does not replace the required strong/moderate/limited synthesis. | **Chapter 9, pp. 137–139; Appendix H, pp. 278, 280.** |
| **6. Mark the evidence basis in Section II** | **“The recommendations below are based on:”** checkboxes. | Select the box or boxes matching the evidence actually used. For **G1**, select **pre-appraised evidence identified in a best-evidence search**; also select **evidence appraised from a targeted search** if eligible supplementary studies contributed. For **G2**, select **evidence appraised by the team from an exhaustive search**. These choices should match Appendix C and the G1/G2 summary. The boxes identify the recommendation's evidence basis; selecting a box does **not** establish the certainty of any individual recommendation. | **Chapter 9, pp. 140, 142; Appendix H, pp. 279, 281.** |
| **7. Draft recommendations that answer the question** | **Section II: Best-Evidence Recommendation(s)** in the applicable certainty rows. | Draft **one specific, concise statement per practice or intervention** that directly answers the EBP question. For **G1**, carry forward the *relevant* recommendations from the pre-appraised source, respecting its stated confidence or certainty; if targeted-search findings are methodologically compelling, record a justified **addition or change** and its implications for certainty. For **G2**, derive each candidate recommendation from the Section I synthesis, including contradictory and null evidence in the reasoning. A statement may favor **or advise against** an intervention when evidence warrants either direction. Write an evidence-level recommendation such as “For `[population]`, `[specific practice]` is `[supported/indicated]` for `[relevant outcome]`,” only when the source(s) support it. Do not turn this field into the local staffing, budget, or implementation plan. Where the evidence cannot support an actionable recommendation, write an uncertainty statement in an appropriate lower-certainty row instead. | **Chapter 9, pp. 139–143; Appendix G1, pp. 266–269; Appendix H, pp. 279, 281–282.** |
| **8. Assign certainty to each recommendation** | Place **each separate recommendation or uncertainty statement** under **High certainty; Reasonable certainty; Reasonable-to-low certainty; or Low certainty**. | Compare the evidence **for that particular statement**: its adequacy, support level, directness for the population and outcome, consistency, quantity, and persuasiveness. For **G1**, interpret the source's own recommendation confidence or grading in light of H's descriptions and update it only if supplementary evidence warrants. For **G2**, use the distribution and synthesis of relevant sources, not a single project-wide grade. Place **robust, consistent, persuasive** support mostly from strong evidence in **High**; **good, mostly consistent** moderate-to-strong support in **Reasonable**; **mixed or conflicting** mostly moderate support in **Reasonable-to-low**; and **minimal, inconsistent, or mainly limited** support in **Low**. In the last two rows, describe what is unresolved and avoid an instruction to implement a practice that lacks adequate support. Document the article numbers and the reason for the tier in working notes so a reader can trace the judgment. A high source count by itself does not establish high certainty. | **Chapter 9, pp. 139–142; Appendix H, pp. 279, 281–282.** |
| **9. Review the completed tool as a team** | Final audit of Section I **when applicable** and Section II. | For a **G2** project, reconcile the three Section I source counts with the included G2 rows, counting each source once in its assigned support group; verify that every synthesis statement's article numbers point to sources that actually support it. For **either pathway**, confirm that Section II's evidence-basis selections match the search; each recommendation answers the same EBP question; its wording reflects the evidence rather than an assumed effect; and **its own** certainty tier is defensible, including any reasonable-to-low or low entries. Resolve reviewer concerns, preserve the final version and rationale, and use it as the **evidence-based starting point** for the subsequent translation phase. | **Chapter 9, pp. 139–143; Appendix G2, pp. 272–273; Appendix H, pp. 278–282.** |

## Writing check

**Source support** describes what an individual source can contribute; **recommendation certainty** describes how well the evidence supports a particular proposed practice. H Section I synthesizes **G2** evidence by support category with source counts and article numbers. H Section II states the correct evidence basis, gives question-answering recommendations, and assigns certainty **recommendation by recommendation**. The tool's lower two rows identify recommendations **lacking adequate evidence**; they should make uncertainty visible rather than imply a practice change is already justified.
