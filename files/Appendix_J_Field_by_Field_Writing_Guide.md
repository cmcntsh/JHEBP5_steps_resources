# What to write in Appendix J: Implementation and Action Planning (A3) Tool

*Johns Hopkins Evidence-Based Practice for Nurses and Healthcare Professionals: Model & Guidelines*, fifth edition (2025)

This guide follows the **eleven-step Appendix J process** in the earlier [Appendix J completion guide](https://chatgpt.com/library?file_id=libfile_f13511a1b0f48191b543aaf0f8e671f7&account_id=272b87f4-0126-403f-8878-95893d50a1d3). These steps are a practical workflow, not separately numbered steps in the textbook. Page numbers are **printed textbook pages**. Replace bracketed examples with verified local information. The A3 is a plan that is **updated during implementation**: enter planned dates now, and actual completion dates and measured results only after they occur.

| Step | Appendix J field or record | What to write | Where to read |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Confirm the change being implemented** | Preparation for the A3; keep **Appendices B, H, and I** beside J. | Identify the exact **EBP question**, the evidence-based recommendation and certainty in **H**, and the **organization-specific decision in I** that the team has agreed to implement. In project notes, state the defined practice, eligible population, setting, and any safety safeguards or feasibility conditions carried forward from I. Example: “Implement `[locally approved practice]` for `[population]` in `[setting]`, subject to `[essential condition]`.” If I recommends deferring or stopping the project, document that decision before drafting an implementation schedule. The A3's subsequent fields should all address the **same selected change**. | **Chapter 9, pp. 139–143; Chapter 10, pp. 149–153; Chapter 11, p. 158; Appendix B, pp. 212–215; Appendix H, pp. 279–282; Appendix I, pp. 286–287; Appendix J, pp. 290, 292.** |
| **2. Fill Problem/Evidence** | A3 **“Problem/Evidence (summary of problem, synthesis of evidence)”**. | Write a brief account of the **local practice gap**, whom it affects, and the **process and patient outcomes** that need improvement. Include dated local baseline data, a denominator or rate definition where relevant, and an organizational or national comparison if available. Then summarize the **question-relevant synthesized evidence** supporting the selected intervention and explain how it could affect the identified process and outcome. **Cite the local data source and the evidence source(s)**; distinguish measured local results from the expected benefit. Example: “During `[period]`, `[numerator/denominator]` of `[eligible events]` met `[process standard]` (`[local report]`). Evidence synthesized in H supports `[practice]` for `[relevant outcome]` (`[source identifiers]`).” Do not invent a baseline, benchmark, or causal effect. | **Chapter 3, pp. 44–50; Chapter 9, pp. 134–139; Chapter 11, pp. 161, 163–164; Appendix J, pp. 290, 292.** |
| **3. Select the implementation framework** | A3 **“Implementation (Educate, Execute)”** and, as useful, the **“Key Accomplishments using Translation Framework”** area. | Name the **framework or model** that will guide this particular change, such as the chapter's Translating Evidence Into Practice (**TRIP**) model if appropriate. State its phases or stages as the organizing headings for the team's work, and identify any complementary improvement tool (for example, a test-of-change cycle) only if the team will actually use it. Briefly explain why the framework fits the setting and change. Example: “Framework: `[name]`; phases: `[phase 1]`, `[phase 2]`, `[phase 3]`; application: `[how phases shape the work]`.” Keep the A3's high-level plan tied to those phases; expand individual tasks later in the work breakdown. | **Chapter 11, pp. 158–162, 164–165; Appendix J, pp. 290, 292.** |
| **4. Set the goal and major accomplishments** | A3 **“SMART Goal aligned with Strategic Priority”** and **“Key Accomplishments using Translation Framework.”** | Write a goal specifying **what will improve, for whom and where, from what baseline to what target, and by when**, using a measure the team can actually collect. Identify the relevant organizational priority if one applies. Example: “By `[date]`, increase `[defined practice measure]` in `[unit]` from `[verified baseline]` to `[target]` among `[eligible cases]`.” Then list each major **deliverable or accomplishment**, grouped by the chosen framework phase: for example, approved protocol, education completed, launch, and evaluation. Name the accountable role for each deliverable. Ensure the goal concerns the problem and the accomplishments make the goal achievable; the work breakdown will later detail the tasks. | **Chapter 11, pp. 161, 163–164; Appendix J, pp. 290, 292.** |
| **5. Identify the people involved** | A3 **“People (Engage)”**: **Project Leader, Working Group, Collaborating Groups, Impacted Groups, Sponsor**. | Enter **names or clearly identified roles** rather than a generic “stakeholders” list. Identify who is accountable for coordinating the project, who performs the work, which groups collaborate, who is affected (including relevant patients, families, or frontline staff), and which leader or body sponsors the improvement. Match the people named here with owners in Key Accomplishments and the work breakdown. State in project notes how each affected group will be engaged or educated if the brief A3 boxes do not provide room. Example: “Project leader: `[role]`; working group: `[roles]`; impacted groups: `[groups]`; sponsor: `[leader/committee]`.” | **Chapter 11, pp. 164–165; Appendix J, pp. 290, 292.** |
| **6. Plan implementation and milestones** | A3 **“Implementation (Educate, Execute)”** and **“Timeline/Milestones (Gantt)”**. | In **Implementation**, describe **what education or communication will prepare staff**, where the approved protocol or supplies will be found, how the new practice will start, and how issues will be reported. In **Timeline/Milestones**, list the major phases or deliverables with **planned start and finish dates or time windows**, including education, launch, baseline measurement when needed, post-implementation data collection, and an evaluation decision. Indicate dependencies between milestones and keep the sequence consistent with the chosen framework. Example: “`[date range]`: train `[roles]`; `[date]`: begin `[practice]`; `[date range]`: collect `[outcome]`; `[date]`: review results.” Use the separate WBS to specify individual tasks; do not record future milestones as already accomplished. | **Chapter 11, pp. 164–165, 167; Appendix J, pp. 290, 292.** |
| **7. Define Metrics Progress (Evaluate)** | A3 **“Metrics Progress (Evaluate)”**. | For each chosen **process measure** (such as adherence or education completion) and **patient/population outcome** (such as an event rate), write the precise measure name, **numerator and denominator or instrument**, data source, eligible population, baseline period and value if known, target, collection schedule, and date for review. Choose measures tied to the goal and distinguish what was **done** from what **happened**. Example: “Process: `[number of eligible encounters with practice documented] / [all eligible encounters]`, from `[audit source]`, weekly; baseline `[verified value]`, target `[goal]` by `[date]`.” For an outcome, state the rate definition and pre/post periods. Add actual post-implementation values only after collection, and record any variation in populations or periods that affects comparison. | **Chapter 11, pp. 163–165; Appendix J, pp. 290, 293.** |
| **8. Record risks and mitigation** | A3 **“Risk and Risk Mitigation Strategy”**: **Risk; Risk Mitigation Strategy; Status**. | Create a separate entry for each material **safety risk or implementation barrier**, using Appendix I's risk assessment and newly identified operational concerns. State the **specific concern**, the action that reduces its likelihood or impact, who will act (in the WBS if needed), and the **current status**. Example: “Risk: `[defined safety or workflow issue]`; mitigation: `[training, protocol check, escalation, monitoring]`; status: `[planned/in progress/resolved]`.” Keep risks distinct from evidence limitations; update status when the mitigation actually occurs. | **Chapter 10, p. 150; Chapter 11, pp. 161, 165; Appendix I, pp. 286–287; Appendix J, pp. 290, 293.** |
| **9. Build the Work Breakdown Structure** | J p. 291 **Work Breakdown Structure**: **Due Date; Task; Dependencies; Accountable Person(s); Status; Planned Completion; Actual Completion; Resource**. | For each milestone or framework phase, list the **individual deliverable-oriented tasks** needed to complete it. In **Due Date**, enter the deadline connected to the A3 timeline; in **Task**, specify an action with an observable result; in **Dependencies**, list approvals or preceding tasks; in **Accountable Person(s)**, assign a named person or role; in **Status**, use a truthful state such as planned, in progress, completed, stalled, or cancelled; in **Planned Completion**, enter the scheduled finish date; in **Actual Completion**, enter the **real date only after completion**; and in **Resource**, identify needed people, policies, applications, supplies, or equipment. Example: “`[due date]` | approve `[protocol]` | after `[review]` | `[owner]` | planned | `[planned finish]` | blank | `[policy committee]`.” A task's due date and planned finish may coincide; make their relationship explicit and keep dates consistent with the A3 milestones. | **Chapter 11, pp. 167–168; Appendix J, WBS p. 291 and instructions pp. 293–294.** |
| **10. Complete the Sustainability Plan** | J p. 291 **“Sustainability Plan (Endure)”**: barriers; mitigations; additional resources; training; continuing responsibilities; owners; additional metrics/outcomes; monitoring frequency. | Fill **each prompt** with a specific long-term arrangement. List what could cause the practice to fade (staff turnover, supply gaps, funding, workflow drift); a corresponding mitigation; any ongoing financial, staffing, or equipment need; refresher and new-employee education; permanent tasks such as policy upkeep or audits; the **person or role** responsible for each; any added measures that will reveal drift or unintended effects; and **how often** the team will collect and review them. Example: “Barrier: `[turnover]`; mitigation: `[include procedure in orientation]`; owner: `[role]`; process audit: `[defined measure]` monthly for `[period]`, then `[review interval]`.” If an item truly does not apply, state why rather than silently leaving a responsibility unassigned. | **Chapter 11, pp. 168–169; Appendix J, sustainability prompts p. 291 and instructions p. 294.** |
| **11. Review and maintain the A3** | Final consistency check across **A3 overview, WBS, and Sustainability Plan**; ongoing status and measured results. | Verify that the **problem and evidence** justify the selected change, the **SMART goal** matches the **metrics**, framework phases match **milestones and WBS tasks**, accountable people are identified, risk mitigations appear in the action plan, and continuing monitoring has an owner. Have the team correct gaps before launch. As implementation proceeds, update **status**, **actual completion dates**, obstacles, and observed measures, then compare results with the baseline and goal to decide whether the plan needs adjustment or wider rollout. Distinguish planned work and anticipated effects from work completed and outcomes actually measured. | **Chapter 11, pp. 164–168; Appendix J, pp. 290–294.** |

## Writing check

Before implementation, a reviewable draft has a **verified problem and evidence summary**, a measurable goal, the chosen framework, named people, a timed set of milestones, defined measures, documented risks, task-level owners and resources, and a sustainability owner and monitoring schedule. **Actual completion dates and post-implementation results remain unfilled until observed.** The overview, WBS, and sustainability plan should describe one coherent change approved in Appendix I.
